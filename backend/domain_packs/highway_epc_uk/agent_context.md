# Highway EPC – UK Smart Motorway / All-Purpose Trunk Road Upgrade

PMO/domain reference — must be reviewed by qualified highway/structural engineers.

## Purpose

This domain pack equips the Agentic PMO Plan, Monitor and Alert agents with a UK-specific reference for delivering a large highway EPC scheme in England (for example an M62 Junction 10–12 Smart Motorway Upgrade, or an A14-class all-purpose trunk road improvement under a DBFO/DPR-type arrangement with National Highways). The goal of such a scheme is to deliver additional capacity and improved journey reliability on the Strategic Road Network (SRN) through an engineered, consenting-compliant and safety-led EPC delivery: all-lane-running smart motorway technology (or trunk road widening), structures renewals, drainage, pavement and technology systems, handed over to the maintainer with a complete as-built and O&M record, on programme and within funding envelope.

## Scope

The pack covers the full eight-phase lifecycle from strategic framing through completion and handover, using National Highways as the design leader/client. It is built on UK standards and statutory frameworks: the Design Manual for Roads and Bridges (DMRB) — including CD 123 (geometric design), CD 236 (pavement design), CD 527 (drainage), CD 377 (road safety audit) — BS 5489 (road lighting), and the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 (CDM 2015). Consenting routes include Development Consent Order (DCO) for NSIPs, Transport and Works Act Order (TWAO), and Town & Country Planning for non-NSIP elements, with Compulsory Purchase Order (CPO) for land.

## National Highways requirements

National Highways (formerly Highways England) is the client and design leader for the SRN. Expect: outcome-based requirements and the Project Control Framework; a robust assurance and project representative (APR) presence; conformity with the DMRB and the Manual of Contract Documents for Highway Works (MCHW); BIM Level 2 information management; a measured, gated approach to consents; and an emphasis on road-user safety, journey-time reliability, carbon reduction and biodiversity net gain. As-builts and the operational technology estate (smart motorway systems) must be handed over in a maintainer-ready state.

## DMRB

The Design Manual for Roads and Bridges is the governing standard set. Key documents referenced in this pack: CD 123 — geometric design of highway infrastructure; CD 236 — pavement design; CD 527 — road drainage; CD 377 — road safety audit (formerly HD 19/15); plus standards for earthworks, structures, environmental assessment and technology. Designs must be demonstrably compliant and deviations require formal Departure from Standard (DFS) approval. Independent Category 2/3 design checks apply to structures.

## CDM 2015

The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 define duty-holders: Client, Principal Designer, Principal Contractor, Designers and Contractors. The pre-construction information, the construction-phase plan and the health and safety file are mandatory deliverables. Risk must be designed out where practicable; residual construction and maintenance risks must be recorded. HSE notification is required for notifiable projects.

## Traffic management

Construction under live traffic is delivered through staged traffic management (TM) designed to the relevant DMRB/MCHW guidance, often within narrow possession windows and frequently overnight under a lane-rental scheme. TM phasing must reconcile programme, safety, road-user delay and access. Diverge/merge tapers, hard-shoulder running, speed limits and emergency-areas must be set correctly. TM is a major source of risk and cost and must be planned and audited.

## Monitoring cadence

- **Weekly:** construction progress, TM/lane-rental status, snagging, HSE near-miss data, weather disruption.
- **Monthly:** cost and schedule performance (CV/CPI, SV/SPI), risk review, KPI dashboard, milestone achievement, procurement status, change control.
- **Phase-gate:** RSA close-out, commissioning acceptance, consent milestones, handover readiness.

## Plan Agent – Critical Red Flags

The Plan Agent must escalate if any of the following is present in the plan:

1. **No ground investigation (GI) task named explicitly** at the start of feasibility and again as a construction-phase GI; ground risk cannot be priced without it.
2. **Consent critical path not isolated.** DCO/TWAO, Public Inquiry, CPO land vesting and utility diversions are discrete long-pole activities — if they are not on the critical path with float, the plan is optimistic.
3. **Long-lead procurement not sequenced before Phase 5.** Structural steel, precast beams, gantries and smart-motorway technology must be ordered against the programme, not after construction starts.
4. **Ecology seasonality ignored.** Great crested newt, bat and bird works are season-constrained; if mitigation and Natural England licensing are not sequenced into the correct window, the plan will slip.
5. **Traffic management staging not planned before mobilisation.** TM phasing, possession windows and lane rental must be designed and accepted before main works.
6. **CDM 2015 duty-holders or construction-phase plan missing.** The plan is non-compliant without them.
7. **No independent design check (Cat 2/3) on structures** or no Stage 1/2/3 Road Safety Audit tasks placed at the correct gates.
8. **Surfacing scheduled without a weather-window check** and without contingency days.
9. **Carbon and BNG targets not allocated** to design and construction tasks.
10. **Handover / as-built / O&M tasks missing or late** in Phase 7.

## Monitor Agent – Critical Red Flags

The Monitor Agent must raise if any of the following is observed:

1. **SPI or CPI dropping below 0.90**, or programme float eroded below the agreed threshold.
2. **Milestone slippage > 30 days** on consent, land or critical-path construction milestones.
3. **Critical-path utility diversion or CPO task** slipping > 2 weeks.
4. **Steel erection / structures progress** more than 10% behind plan.
5. **Surfacing weather-disruption days** exceeding the allowance, or the window closing.
6. **Lane-rental budget consumed above 80%** before completion of TM.
7. **Quality first-time-yield or compaction pass-rate** below target, or rework rate rising.
8. **Smart-motorway integration test failures** or lighting energisation slipping.
9. **VRS non-conformances** raised, or a system not certified.
10. **As-built data incomplete** for completed structures before handover.

## Alert Agent – Critical Red Flags

The Alert Agent must fire a critical alert on:

1. **Public Inquiry adjourned or DCO determination delayed** beyond the planned date.
2. **GCN/bat mitigation season window closing** with the licence or works late.
3. **Pavement surfacing weather window missed**, with consequent re-sequencing and cost.
4. **Long-lead items not ordered** before the Phase 5 start, threatening the critical path.
5. **CPO objection blocking land access** required for an imminent construction possession.
6. **HV cable strike / serious near-miss** during excavation under TM.
7. **Steel/bitumen price escalation** moving CPI materially below target.
8. **Snagging closure rate below 60%** approaching a Stage 3 RSA or handover gate.
