# Playbook — Board Diversity & Diversity Policy Engagement

A phase-gate lifecycle for a Board Diversity & Governance advisory engagement. Each phase has
an **objective**, **key activities**, and **exit criteria** (the conditions that must be met
before opening the next gate). Progress is governed against the WBS, risk register, monitoring
rules, and KPI definitions that accompany this pack.

> **Governance note.** Engagements with listed-company boards are sensitive and confidential.
> Board composition data, diversity breakdowns, director evaluation notes, and succession
> candidates are legally and reputationally sensitive and are frequently subject to NDAs,
> listing-rule disclosure rules, and data-protection law (e.g. UK GDPR special-category
> considerations around ethnicity and health). Never share board-level data outside the agreed
> data-access ring-fence; default to aggregated reporting; and document every disclosure
> decision. A typical board appointment cycle runs **6–12 months** — never promise meaningful
> composition change on a shorter horizon.

---

## Phase 0 — Strategic Framing
**Objective:** Establish a clear, signed-off mandate, ring-fenced data access, and a realistic plan before any board data is touched.

**Key activities:**
- Draft the project charter: objectives, scope boundaries, success criteria, timeline, budget.
- Conduct stakeholder mapping (board chair, nomination committee chair, company secretary, GC/legal, HR, investor relations).
- Secure the **data access agreement** and **confidentiality protocol** for board-level data.
- Run an initial **governance assessment** (current code, listing venue, AGM cycle).
- Build the RACI matrix, communication plan, and risk register baseline.
- Scan the regulatory landscape applicable to the client.
- Confirm the board sponsor and run the kick-off workshop.

**Exit criteria:** Charter signed by executive sponsor and board chair; data access agreement executed; sponsor confirmed; team assembled.

---

## Phase 1 — Board & Policy Discovery
**Objective:** Build an evidence base from documents and elite interviews — the current state of the board, its policies, and its processes.

**Key activities:**
- Analyse **current board composition** (gender, ethnicity, age, tenure, skills, independence, nationality).
- Review board/committee charters and Terms of Reference, current diversity policy, prior board evaluation outputs, ESG/disclosure reports, and any prior diversity initiatives.
- Collect HR diversity data and review the succession plan.
- Conduct **elite interviews** with the board chair, nomination committee chair, and CEO; distribute a board self-assessment questionnaire.
- Complete compliance checks against the UK Corporate Governance Code, the Parker Review, and Hampton-Alexander / FTSE Women Leaders Review targets.

**Exit criteria:** Baseline composition dataset validated against ≥2 sources; interview transcripts coded; compliance position documented; data gaps logged as risks.

---

## Phase 2 — Diagnostic & Benchmarking
**Objective:** Convert raw data into a quantified diagnosis with peer and regulatory benchmarking and a clear gap analysis.

**Key activities:**
- Run gender, ethnicity, age/tenure, skills-matrix, cognitive-diversity, and independence analyses.
- Benchmark against FTSE 350 and FTSE 250 peers and sector comparators.
- Perform historical trend analysis and **gap analysis** against regulatory targets.
- Conduct **intersectionality analysis** (gender × ethnicity) and a diversity-maturity assessment.
- Produce the board effectiveness scorecard and nomination committee effectiveness review; assemble the benchmark report.

**Exit criteria:** Benchmark report accepted by sponsor; gaps quantified and prioritised; benchmarking methodology signed off (see commissioning checklist).

---

## Phase 3 — Policy & Process Design
**Objective:** Turn the diagnosis into a defensible, board-ready set of policies, processes, targets, and frameworks.

**Key activities:**
- Design the **diversity policy framework**, board appointment policy, and updated nomination committee charter.
- Define the **target-setting methodology** (quotas vs voluntary targets — with rationale).
- Design the board evaluation framework, succession-planning framework, and board-ready candidate pipeline.
- Design mentorship and sponsorship programmes and inclusive board culture guidelines.
- Produce the disclosure/reporting framework, regulatory compliance roadmap, board skills-matrix update, and diversity data-governance framework; prioritise recommendations into an implementation roadmap.

**Exit criteria:** Draft policies and frameworks reviewed by legal; recommendations prioritised and costed; ready for stakeholder consultation.

---

## Phase 4 — Stakeholder Engagement & Consultation
**Objective:** Build the consensus and approvals required for adoption by testing recommendations with the board, leadership, and investors.

**Key activities:**
- Present findings to the full board; run the nomination committee workshop and leadership briefing.
- Develop the shareholder engagement strategy and align with investor stewardship expectations.
- Consult the relevant regulator where applicable; run a feedback-integration workshop.
- Secure policy-wording approval; evaluate the board-effectiveness pilot; present the candidate pipeline.

**Exit criteria:** Board and nomination committee endorse the policy and roadmap; investor feedback integrated; policy wording approved.

---

## Phase 5 — Implementation & Change
**Objective:** Move from endorsed policy to lived practice — adoption, pipeline activation, programmes, and reporting infrastructure.

**Key activities:**
- Secure **policy adoption** by board resolution and AGM resolution preparation as needed.
- Adopt the nomination committee process and align the board appointment cycle.
- Support candidate search and selection; launch mentorship and sponsorship programmes.
- Integrate the board evaluation cycle; stand up diversity data-collection infrastructure and reporting automation.
- Communicate the policy internally and externally (annual report disclosure); run culture-change initiatives.

**Exit criteria:** Policy adopted and minuted; first diverse appointments/candidates in process; reporting infrastructure live; internal/external communications issued.

---

## Phase 6 — Monitoring & Measurement
**Objective:** Make diversity and effectiveness visible, comparable, and accountable over time.

**Key activities:**
- Deploy the KPI dashboard and diversity scorecard; establish the diversity metrics baseline.
- Track targets against milestones; monitor policy compliance, board effectiveness, and candidate pipeline.
- Run retention analysis and **glass cliff monitoring indicators**.
- Establish the quarterly reporting cadence and provide annual report disclosure support.

**Exit criteria:** Dashboard live with baseline + targets; first quarterly progress report issued; compliance monitoring operational.

---

## Phase 7 — Review & Continuous Improvement
**Objective:** Embed a sustainable cycle of evaluation, learning, and refinement — and hand capability to the client.

**Key activities:**
- Conduct the annual board effectiveness review and policy effectiveness audit.
- Run the stakeholder feedback survey and regulatory-change impact assessment.
- Refine the roadmap; capture lessons learned.
- Produce the next-phase recommendation, engagement close-out report, and knowledge transfer / capability handover to the governance team.

**Exit criteria:** Close-out report accepted; lessons-learned workshop held; capability transferred; next-phase recommendation agreed (or engagement formally closed).

---

### Cross-phase principles
- **Evidence over assertion.** Every recommendation traces to Phase 1/2 data.
- **Confidentiality by default.** Aggregate before you disclose; log every sharing decision.
- **Comply-or-explain discipline.** Document either compliance *or* a credible explanation.
- **No glass cliff.** Diverse appointments must be made into stable, supported roles — not crisis trapdoors.
- **Intersectional, not unidimensional.** Track gender × ethnicity × tenure, not gender alone.
